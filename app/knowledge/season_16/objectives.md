# Season 16 Objectives and Macro

Last updated: September 2026
Applies to: Standard Summoner's Rift
Primary sources: Riot Games Patch 25.09, 26.1, 26.11 and current objective rules

# Quick Reference

- Objective value is contextual: do not choose Dragon, Herald, Baron, or another objective purely because it is available.
- Evaluate current team strength, numbers, vision, lane priority, enemy location, objective rewards, Soul state, composition, Smite availability, and available cross-map resources.
- Rift Herald is both an objective and a tempo/map-pressure tool; its value increases when it converts into plates/turrets or forces responses before another major objective.
- Herald can be used before Dragon or another major fight to create pressure on the opposite side of the map and force an enemy response.
- Avoid wasting Herald on a structure that would fall naturally when another lane can convert it into substantially more gold or map access.
- Baron becomes more attractive when your team controls vision, can kill it quickly, has sufficient health/DPS, and can prevent or survive an enemy contest.
- Dragon becomes more valuable as a team approaches Soul and when the Soul is particularly valuable for either composition.
- Baron can be preferable to Dragon when Dragon's immediate strategic value is low and your team has a strong, safe Baron window.
- Giving Dragon can be correct if contesting requires entering enemy-controlled terrain from a losing position and a higher-value cross-map trade is available.
- Before starting an objective, ask what happens if the enemy arrives: turn, finish, disengage, or zone.
- Secure lane priority and vision before the objective whenever possible rather than arriving only when it spawns.
- Use the detailed sections below for exact spawn timers, rewards, objective mechanics, Herald usage, and Baron-vs-Dragon decision rules.

# Purpose

Objectives should not be treated as automatic "must contest" events.

When deciding whether to take, contest, trade, or ignore an objective, evaluate:

- objective reward
- spawn timing
- current team strength
- lane priority
- vision control
- jungler / Smite status
- time required to take the objective
- nearby turret value
- wave value
- death timers
- team composition
- future objective timers
- whether the reward is temporary or permanent

Season 16 intentionally made epic monsters slower and riskier to take while increasing the value of turret pressure.

Therefore:

"Objective is alive"
does NOT mean
"team must group for it."

# Season 16 Epic Monster Durability

Patch 26.1 intentionally made epic monsters harder to burst.

The general objective was to increase effective kill time by approximately:

10-30%

depending on the monster and game state.

This matters because starting an objective creates a longer vulnerability window.

Longer objective time means:

- more time for enemies to rotate
- more time for flank angles to develop
- more health lost while taking the objective
- more time spent away from waves and turrets
- greater danger of a Smite contest

True damage sources such as Smite are not reduced by normal Armor or Magic Resistance.

Therefore upgraded Smite remains especially important.

# ELEMENTAL DRAGONS

# Basic Rewards

Each Elemental Dragon grants:

75 gold to the killer

Local XP:

approximately 160-400 depending on level

The more important reward is the permanent team-wide Dragon Slayer buff.

Each Dragon buff persists through death.

---

# Dragon Sequence

First Dragon:

random element

Second Dragon:

different element

After the second Dragon dies:

the Rift transforms based on the third Dragon's element.

From the third Dragon onward:

only the dominant Rift element spawns.

The first team to reach four total Dragon kills receives:

Dragon Soul

matching the dominant element.

Dragon Soul is:

- permanent
- team-wide
- retained through death

---

# Dragon Vengeance

Season 16 substantially increased Dragon durability against teams that are already ahead in the Dragon race.

Dragon Vengeance damage reduction:

15%
30%
45%

depending on how far the attacking team has progressed toward Soul.

This means later Dragons become much harder for the team approaching Soul to kill.

The purpose is to make Soul-point Dragons highly contestable.

## Macro Implication

A team on three Dragons should NOT assume that the fourth Dragon can be rushed as easily as the first.

Soul-point Dragon requires:

- earlier setup
- stronger vision
- more time
- safer positioning
- better enemy-jungler control

Because the Dragon takes longer to kill, an enemy team that is behind has more opportunity to contest or steal it.

---

# Dragon Soul Value

Dragon Soul is one of the highest-value permanent objectives in the game.

Unlike Baron:

Dragon Soul does NOT expire.

Unlike Elder:

Dragon Soul is NOT lost on death.

The reward persists for the rest of the game.

Therefore Soul should generally be treated as much more important than an individual early Dragon.

---

# Dragon Priority

## First Dragon

Usually moderate priority.

It is often correct to trade the first Dragon for:

- multiple turret plates
- Void Grubs
- a turret
- a large top-side advantage
- several jungle camps
- a successful cross-map play

Do not force a losing fight for first Dragon.

---

## Second Dragon

Still often tradeable.

However, the value depends on the Dragon race.

Example:

Enemy has:

1 Dragon

Your team has:

0

Losing the second Dragon gives the enemy:

2-0 Dragon lead

This begins creating future Soul pressure.

---

## Third Dragon / Soul Point

If one team reaches:

3 Dragons

the next Dragon becomes:

Soul point.

This dramatically increases its strategic value.

A team facing enemy Soul point should prepare earlier than for a normal Dragon.

---

## Fourth Dragon / Soul

If this Dragon grants Soul:

priority is extremely high.

Giving Soul should usually require receiving very large compensation elsewhere.

Examples that may justify giving Soul:

- guaranteed Baron plus multiple turrets
- immediate game-ending push
- impossible-to-win contest
- multiple players already dead
- no realistic access to the pit

Do NOT recommend walking into an unwinnable 4v5 simply because the Dragon is Soul.

---

# When to Take Dragon

Good conditions:

- Bot lane has priority
- Mid lane has priority
- enemy jungler is dead
- enemy jungler is visible top-side
- enemy team recently recalled
- your team has better objective damage
- your jungler has Smite
- enemy team cannot enter river safely
- your team wins the likely fight

---

# When NOT to Take Dragon

Avoid starting when:

- both nearby lanes are pushed into your team
- enemy team has clear vision control
- enemy jungler is nearby and alive
- your jungler has no Smite
- multiple teammates are low
- important ultimates are unavailable
- your composition takes Dragon extremely slowly
- enemy team has strong engage into the pit

# VOID GRUBS

## Spawn

8:00

Single encounter.

Three Grubs.

They do not respawn.

---

# Gold

Each Grub:

30 local gold

Only the killer receives the gold.

Full camp:

90 total local gold

There is no team-wide global gold reward.

---

# XP

Each Grub:

65 XP

All three:

195 XP

XP radius:

2000

---

# Grub Buff

Killing Void Grubs grants stacks that improve turret damage.

The Grub system is intended to increase structure pressure.

Three Grubs unlock:

Hunger of the Void

which allows summoned Voidmites to assist turret pushes.

Patch 26.11 increased the strength of this pushing reward.

Current summoned Voidmite health:

100% of a melee minion's health

rather than the previous 60%.

This makes the three-Grub reward more useful for sustained turret pressure.

---

# Macro Value of Grubs

Grubs are primarily a:

turret-pressure objective

not a large immediate-gold objective.

The killer earns only:

30 gold per Grub

so laners should not sacrifice large waves merely to stand near the camp.

Season 16 specifically moved the reward away from global gold to reduce unnecessary full-team rotations.

---

# When Grubs Are Worth Taking

Good situations:

- enemy jungler is bot-side
- Top and Mid have priority
- Dragon is not worth contesting
- your composition is strong at split pushing
- your team has champions that heavily pressure turrets
- a side lane is likely to snowball
- Grubs can be taken quickly without sacrificing lane state

---

# When to Trade Grubs for Dragon

Trading Grubs for Dragon is completely reasonable.

Example:

Enemy commits Bot + Jungle + Mid to Dragon.

Your team has strong Top priority.

Possible response:

take Grubs
+
take Top plates
+
invade top jungle

This may produce more guaranteed gold and pressure than contesting a losing Dragon fight.

# RIFT HERALD

## Spawn

15:00

## Despawn

Approximately:

19:45

if not in combat.

Baron replaces Herald at:

20:00

---

# Herald Kill Reward

Gold:

100 gold to killer

XP:

240

XP radius:

2000

The direct gold reward is intentionally small.

The real reward is:

Eye of the Herald

---

# Eye of the Herald

Eye duration:

240 seconds

= 4 minutes

If not used within that time:

the Eye expires.

This means Herald should usually be taken with a plan for where to use it.

---

# Herald Charge Damage

First structure charge:

3000 damage

Season 16 removed the previous level-dependent optimization.

You do NOT need to delay summoning Herald to increase first-charge damage.

---

# Macro Value of Herald

Herald is not primarily a:

"100 gold objective."

Its value comes from converting into:

- plates
- turrets
- map access
- wave pressure
- future objective control

The correct question is:

"What structure can Herald unlock?"

---

# Herald and Turret Gold

Example:

A Herald charge breaks three 120g plates.

Plate value:

3 × 120
=
360 local gold

plus:

100g Herald kill reward

plus:

possible turret destruction gold

plus:

wave denial

plus:

map access

This is much more valuable than thinking of Herald as only a 100g monster.

---

# Best Herald Uses

## 1. Finish a Nearly Dead Turret

This is often the most reliable use.

Benefits:

- guarantees turret destruction
- gives immediate gold
- opens the lane
- denies enemy protection from that turret
- makes future rotations easier

---

## 2. Open Mid Turret

Mid Tier 1 is often the most strategically valuable first turret to destroy.

Destroying Mid Tier 1:

- opens access to both sides of enemy jungle
- makes deep vision easier
- allows Mid to rotate more freely
- increases Dragon and Baron access
- reduces safety in the center of the map

If several lanes are viable Herald targets:

Mid turret often has the highest map-control value.

---

## 3. Accelerate a Winning Side Lane

If Top or Bot has a large lead:

Herald can turn lane advantage into:

- Tier 1
- Tier 2 access
- deeper jungle control
- side-lane pressure

This can force multiple opponents to respond later.

---

# Herald Before a Major Objective

One of the strongest uses is:

Herald approximately 60-90 seconds before Dragon or Baron

in a side or mid lane.

Example:

Dragon spawns in 60 seconds.

Use Herald Mid.

Enemy must choose:

defend turret
or
move to Dragon.

If they defend:

your team can establish river first.

If they ignore Herald:

they lose structure gold and map control.

This creates:

forced map pressure

before the objective.

---

# Herald and Tempo

Do NOT drop Herald immediately merely because you have it.

Ask:

- which turret has the most plate value?
- which lane can actually protect Herald?
- which turret unlocks the most map?
- what major objective is spawning next?
- can Herald force an enemy rotation?
- is the Eye close to expiring?

---

# Herald for Maximum Gold

If optimizing immediate gold:

prefer a turret with:

- several available plates
- low enough HP that Herald can threaten a full kill
- allied minions already arriving
- low enemy resistance

Example:

Tier 2 turret with three plates remaining:

3 × 120
=
360 local plate gold

If Herald charge plus follow-up destroys the turret:

this can produce a very large guaranteed conversion.

---

# Herald for Map Control

If optimizing map state instead of personal gold:

Mid Tier 1
or
an inhibitor-access turret

may be more valuable.

A lower-gold turret can still be the correct Herald target if destroying it gives:

- deeper vision
- easier Baron access
- easier Dragon access
- stronger side-lane pressure
- enemy jungle entry

---

# Herald Mistakes

Avoid:

- using Herald into a defended turret with no minion wave
- dropping it immediately after capture with no purpose
- allowing Eye to expire
- using Herald on a low-value turret while a major map-opening turret is available
- forcing a Herald push when your team should be setting up a decisive objective

Hand of Baron

650 per allied player

3250 team XP

# Baron Durability

Season 16 Baron is significantly harder to rush.

Minimum level:

11

Approximate HP range:

17,792-19,190+

Armor:

104-170 depending on level

Magic Resistance:

67-100

---

# Baron Contest Risk

While fighting Baron:

Baron shreds:

0.5 Armor
and
0.5 Magic Resistance

per second

from nearby enemies.

This makes a team actively taking Baron increasingly vulnerable if enemies arrive.

Therefore:

being "ahead" does NOT automatically make starting Baron safe.

---

# Hand of Baron

Duration:

180 seconds

= 3 minutes

Lost on death.

Provides:

- bonus AD
- bonus AP
- Empowered Recall
- empowered nearby allied minions

Baron is primarily a:

siege and map-pressure buff.

---

# When Baron Is Worth Taking

Strong Baron conditions include:

## Enemy Jungler Dead

One of the strongest Baron windows.

Without enemy Smite:

objective-steal risk falls dramatically.

---

## Multiple Enemies Dead

Example:

enemy Jungle + Mid dead

for 30+ seconds.

This can create a clean Baron window.

---

## Enemy Shows Far Away

Example:

enemy ADC appears Bot with no Teleport.

Your team is already around Baron.

Potential:

5v4 or 5v3 Baron-side numbers advantage.

---

## Strong Baron DPS

Compositions with:

- high sustained DPS
- strong on-hit damage
- tanks that can absorb Baron
- strong healing/shielding

can take Baron much faster.

Examples of champions that usually increase Baron speed:

- ADCs
- Cassiopeia
- Azir
- Heimerdinger
- Master Yi
- Bel'Veth
- Karthus
- strong sustained-damage junglers

Do not assume all compositions can Baron equally quickly.

---

# When Baron Is Dangerous

Avoid starting when:

- enemy jungler is alive and nearby
- your team takes Baron slowly
- enemy has strong AoE engage
- lanes are pushing into your base
- you have no vision over entrances
- several teammates are below half HP
- key ultimates are unavailable

---

# Baron Vision Setup

Before Baron:

Push:

Top
and
Mid

Then establish vision around:

- Baron pit
- river entrances
- enemy jungle entrances
- Blast Cone access
- nearby Faelights

Clear enemy wards.

Control Wards are particularly important inside Baron pit.

---

# Baron vs Dragon

This decision should NOT be based solely on:

"Baron > Dragon"

or

"Dragon > Baron."

Evaluate what each reward does.

---

# Baron vs Early Dragon

If the available Dragon is:

first
or
second Dragon

and Baron is safely available:

Baron is often more valuable.

Why:

Baron can immediately convert into:

- Tier 2 turrets
- inhibitors
- map control
- large gold swings
- ending pressure

An early individual Dragon only contributes one permanent stack.

---

# Baron vs Soul-Point Dragon

More complicated.

Example:

Enemy has 3 Dragons.

Dragon spawning would give them Soul.

If your team takes Baron while enemy takes Soul:

you are trading:

temporary 3-minute siege power

for:

permanent enemy Soul.

This is often dangerous.

Whether Baron is worth it depends on:

- Soul type
- how quickly Baron can be taken
- whether Baron can immediately end the game
- team scaling
- current gold lead
- enemy defensive strength

---

# When Baron Can Be Better Than Soul

Baron may be preferable if:

- you can take Baron uncontested
- your team is already far ahead
- Baron will immediately produce inhibitors or Nexus pressure
- enemy cannot defend against empowered siege
- your composition is excellent at sieging
- enemy Soul is relatively less impactful for the current compositions
- contesting Dragon would require a dangerous face-check

---

# When Soul Is Better Than Baron

Prefer Soul when:

- game is expected to continue for a long time
- your composition scales well
- Soul strongly complements your team
- Baron push is unlikely to break the base
- enemy waveclear is very strong
- taking Baron would expose your team to a steal
- Dragon fight is much safer

Soul is permanent.

Baron lasts:

180 seconds.

That difference matters.

---

# Team Composition Considerations

## Strong Siege Composition

Examples:

- long-range poke
- ADC with strong turret damage
- strong waveclear denial
- split-push pressure

Baron becomes more valuable.

---

## Strong Teamfight Composition

If your team is stronger in direct fights:

Dragon Soul may be more valuable because its power persists in every future fight.

---

## Poor Siege Composition

If your team struggles to hit turrets even with Baron:

Soul may be the more reliable long-term reward.

---

## Strong Split-Push Composition

Baron can be extremely valuable.

Empowered minions in multiple lanes create:

1-3-1
or
4-1

pressure.

---

# Baron Bait

Sometimes the correct Baron play is:

not actually taking Baron.

Start or threaten Baron to force enemies into fog of war.

Then:

turn and fight.

This is especially effective when:

- your team has strong engage
- enemies must face-check
- you control vision
- enemy jungler is forced to approach

# Elder Durability

Minimum level:

13

Approximate HP:

17,796-21,275+

122-170

Magic Resistance:

76-100

Elder is extremely durable.

---

# Aspect of the Dragon

Duration:

150 seconds

Lost on death.

Effects include:

- true-damage burn on attacks and abilities
- execute against enemy champions below 20% HP

The execute makes Elder one of the strongest fight-ending buffs in the game.

---

# Elder Priority

Elder should usually be treated as:

higher priority than Baron

because it directly decides champion combat.

However:

a team can still trade Elder for an immediate Nexus if the game can be ended before the enemy uses the buff.

---

# Baron vs Elder

If both are available:

Elder usually has higher immediate combat value.

Baron:

better at siege and waves.

Elder:

better at winning fights.

If your team can safely take Elder:

prioritize it in most late-game states.

---

# Possible Baron-for-Elder Trade

Usually bad if:

enemy gets Elder
and
your Baron team cannot immediately end.

Why:

Elder can allow the enemy to kill Baron carriers and erase the Baron advantage.

Baron is lost on death.

---

# Objective Setup Timing

For important objectives:

begin preparation approximately:

45-90 seconds before spawn.

Ideal sequence:

1. Push nearby lanes.
2. Recall.
3. Spend gold.
4. Move toward objective.
5. Establish vision.
6. Clear enemy vision.
7. Control entrances.
8. Start only when conditions are favorable.

---

# Wave Timing Before Objectives

Approximately 60 seconds before objective:

start planning the relevant lane wave.

Example:

Dragon in 60 seconds.

Push Mid.
Push Bot.

Then recall.

If done correctly:

enemy must catch waves

while your team moves into river first.

---

# Cross-Map Objective Trading

When an objective cannot be contested:

do not arrive late and die.

Trade for:

- opposite-side turret
- plates
- Herald
- Grubs
- enemy jungle camps
- waves
- vision
- Baron
- Dragon

depending on the game state.

---

# Objective Expected Value

When deciding whether to contest:

Expected Objective Value =
probability of winning
× reward

minus:

- expected deaths
- lost waves
- lost turret gold
- lost tempo
- risk of steal

This explains why a theoretically powerful objective can still be incorrect to contest.

---

Enemy starts first Dragon.

3 Tier 2 plates

3 × 120

360 guaranteed local gold

plus:

wave gold

plus:

turret damage

plus:

possible Crystalline Overgrowth

- losing the wave

# Example: Soul vs Tier 2 Turret

Enemy is taking Soul.

one Tier 2 turret

600 local plate gold

Unless:

the contest is impossible
or
the turret push can immediately transition into ending the game

Soul should receive much higher priority.

# Example: Baron vs Third Dragon

enemy is NOT receiving Soul.

---

# Example: Baron vs Enemy Soul

Enemy has 3 Dragons.

Your team can either:

Baron
or
contest Dragon.

- can Baron be finished before Dragon?
- can Baron end the game?
- what Soul is spawning?

- who has better siege?
