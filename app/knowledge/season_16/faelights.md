# Season 16 Faelights and Vision

Last updated: September 2026
Applies to: Standard Summoner's Rift
Primary sources: Riot Games Patch 26.1, Patch 26.3, and Elemental Rift rules

# Quick Refence

- Faelights are a Season 16 vision system that changes how teams establish safe territory and identify gank paths.
- Faelights do not become available immediately; they appear after the second Dragon has been slain.
- Faelight control should be treated as part of your safe pushing boundary: deeper reliable vision allows a side laner to pressure farther forward before becoming vulnerable to unseen rotations.
- A lit Faelight does not make the entire surrounding area safe; identify paths and terrain that remain outside its vision.
- Enemies can deliberately path through remaining vision gaps, so combine Faelight information with lane state, missing champions, objective state, and ordinary wards.
- High-value Faelights near important rotation routes or contested territory can justify a Control Ward when clearing that vision would force the enemy to enter dangerous space.
- Regular wards are preferable when the vision is temporary, easily replaceable, or the Control Ward would be trivially cleared.
- Control Wards should not be placed merely because a Faelight location exists; consider how safely the enemy can reach and clear the ward.
- Before extending in a side lane, identify which enemy approaches are covered by Faelights/wards and which approaches remain dark.
- Before major objectives, use Faelights together with conventional wards to establish multiple layers of information rather than relying on a single vision source.
- For exact locations, vision gaps, Control Ward priorities, and map-specific examples, use the detailed sections below.

# Purpose

Faelights are fixed map locations that enhance wards and create temporary extended vision.

They are designed to help players:

- push side lanes more safely
- establish objective vision
- identify jungle rotations
- defend territory
- create vision without requiring a support to place several wards

They do NOT make an area completely safe.

There are intentional gaps between Faelight vision regions so junglers and roaming champions can still move around them.

---

# Faelight Core Mechanics

Placing ANY ward on a Faelight creates a Faelight Superward.

This includes:

- Stealth Wards
- Control Wards
- support-item wards
- other normal ward types

A Faelight Superward gains:

+25% normal vision radius

and also reveals a separate location-specific bonus vision region for:

45 seconds

The bonus region:

- has a different shape depending on the Faelight location
- can reveal brush if that brush lies inside the bonus region
- is only visually displayed to the allied team
- disappears when the ward is destroyed
- is NOT larger when using a Control Ward instead of a normal ward

Therefore:

Control Ward
does NOT
increase the Faelight vision shape or duration.

The reason to use a Control Ward on a Faelight is persistence and vision denial, not stronger Faelight vision.

---

# Enemy Awareness

Enemies are not automatically shown the outline of the bonus Faelight vision region.

They can learn they are inside it when:

- a Sweeper / detector effect is active in the region
- their team directly ping-tracks the ward
- effects such as Sixth Sense identify the ward

This can make Faelight vision deceptive.

A player may believe they are walking through fog while actually being seen by the bonus region.

---

# Ward Replacement Rule

Only one allied ward can occupy a Faelight.

If an allied ward already exists on that Faelight and another allied ward is placed there:

the old ward is destroyed

and replaced by the new one.

Do not accidentally replace a valuable Control Ward with a temporary Stealth Ward.

---

# Faelight Timeline

## At Game Start

8 Faelights are already present:

4 near base gates

2 in side-lane island brushes

2 in river-wall / banana brushes near mid

These exist before any Dragon dies.

---

# After Elemental Rift Transformation

4 additional Faelights appear:

one near each side lane in each jungle quadrant.

These are the major split-push Faelights.

In standard Summoner's Rift:

Elemental Rift transformation occurs after the second Elemental Drake dies.

Therefore these 4 side-lane jungle-path Faelights appear after the second Dragon is killed.

The map then transforms into the element of the third Dragon.

Total Faelights after transformation:

12

---

# GAME-START FAELIGHT LOCATIONS

# 1. Side-Lane Island Brush Faelights

Number:

2 total

Locations:

- island brush near Top lane
- island brush near Bot lane

Purpose:

provide extended river vision during laning.

These are intentionally simple, high-value laning wards.

## What They Help See

Depending on side and map orientation, these can reveal:

- river movement
- Scuttle area approaches
- jungler movement entering the lane from river
- roaming Mid or Support movement
- portions of common river paths

## What They Do NOT Reliably Cover

They do not eliminate:

- lane ganks
- jungle entrances behind you
- blast-cone routes
- wall-crossing champions
- Teleport flanks
- movement through paths outside the shaped bonus region

Therefore:

"Island Faelight warded"
does NOT mean
"you can push to enemy turret with no risk."

---

# How Far Can You Play With Island Faelight Vision?

## Early Laning

If:

- river Faelight is warded
- enemy laner is visible
- enemy jungler is expected to approach from river
- no major lane-gank route exists

then you can generally play farther forward than with no river vision.

However, if the wave is near the enemy turret, the jungler may still:

- enter through the lane
- path through tri-brush
- come from behind
- use mobility across terrain

The deeper you push, the more you need additional information.

---

# Island Brush Control Ward Value

Usually:

REGULAR WARD preferred

during normal early laning.

Why:

- the location is high traffic
- enemies frequently walk through river
- the ward is relatively easy to sweep or attack
- placing a visible Control Ward can give the enemy an easy 30g clear

Use a Control Ward here when:

- your team actively controls the nearby river
- you need persistent objective setup
- your jungler / support can defend it
- an upcoming Dragon or Grub fight makes persistent denial valuable

Do NOT default to putting a Control Ward here simply because it is a Faelight.

---

# 2. Mid River-Wall / "Banana Brush" Faelights

Number:

2 total

Location:

one on each side of Mid lane near the river-wall brush.

These hug the Mid-lane brush but do not overlap it.

Their purpose is:

- detecting river rotations
- protecting Mid from river approaches
- improving access to objective setup
- tracking Jungle / Support movement

---

# Patch 26.3 Nerf

These Faelights were TOO strong at Season 16 launch.

Originally their bonus vision extended:

past the Scuttle area
and
toward epic-monster pit entrances.

Patch 26.3 reduced their bonus vision shape.

Current behavior:

the bonus region cuts off BEFORE:

- the Scuttle pad
- the epic-objective pit entrance

This means one Mid Faelight does NOT provide full river/objective control.

Do NOT assume it sees Dragon or Baron entrance simply because it sees part of river.

---

# Vision Gaps Around Mid Faelights

Major gaps can exist:

- beyond the Scuttle area
- around objective-pit entrances
- through opposite-side jungle entrances
- over walls
- through lane-side paths
- between the Faelight region and deeper river wards

This is intentional.

Patch 26.3 specifically reduced the ability for Mid laners to completely zone junglers using one safe ward.

---

# Mid Faelight Control Ward Value

Usually:

REGULAR WARD for laning information

CONTROL WARD situationally for objective control.

Control Ward is stronger when:

- Dragon / Baron setup is beginning
- your team controls Mid priority
- Support/Jungle can protect the ward
- denying enemy river vision matters

It is weaker when:

- Mid has no priority
- enemy Jungle/Support can walk up freely
- the ward will be cleared immediately

A visible Control Ward in a central river location is often easy for the enemy to remove if you cannot contest them.

---

# 3. Base-Gate Faelights

Number:

4 total

One near each major base gate.

Purpose:

provide vision into nearby jungle paths.

These become increasingly relevant when:

- one team is pushed into base
- the winning team is invading
- Baron/Elder setup reaches deep territory
- defenders need safe information while leaving base

Riot intentionally designed these to be easier for the losing team to retake.

---

# Defensive Use

For the defending team, base-gate Faelights can help detect:

- enemy invades
- jungle traps
- rotations between lanes
- enemies waiting outside base

This reduces the risk of blindly walking into your own jungle.

---

# Offensive Use

The winning team can use them for:

- tracking defenders leaving base
- controlling enemy jungle
- preparing picks
- protecting deep side-lane pressure

However:

these are less permanent offensive positions than deep side-lane Faelights.

The defending team is geographically close to them.

---

# Base-Gate Control Ward Value

## Defending Team

CONTROL WARD can be valuable.

Reasons:

- easy for your team to defend
- close to base
- enemies take significant risk walking deep to clear it
- provides persistent denial near your own jungle entrance

This is one of the better defensive Control Ward locations.

## Attacking Team

Usually less reliable.

A deep Control Ward near enemy base can be valuable while:

- your team is sieging
- enemies are trapped inside base
- your team controls surrounding jungle

But once pressure disappears:

the enemy can often clear it safely.

---

# POST-TRANSFORMATION SIDE-LANE FAELIGHTS

# 4. Side-Lane Jungle-Path Faelights

Number:

4 total

One in each jungle quadrant near a side lane.

They appear only after:

Elemental Rift transformation

which occurs after the second Elemental Dragon dies in standard Summoner's Rift.

These are the most important Faelights for split pushing.

Riot specifically designed them to help a side-laner create enough vision to make a play without requiring several support wards.

---

# What They Reveal

Each one provides bonus vision along an important jungle path adjacent to the side lane.

Their purpose is to detect enemies rotating from jungle toward the lane.

They are especially useful when:

- Top laner is pushing deeply
- ADC is collecting a long side wave
- split pusher pressures Tier 2 / Tier 3
- team is applying 1-3-1 or 4-1 pressure

---

# How Faelights Change Safe Side-Lane Depth

Without vision:

pushing beyond river is often dangerous when multiple enemies are missing.

With the relevant side-lane Faelight active:

the split pusher gains earlier warning of enemies approaching through the covered jungle path.

This may allow the player to pressure:

- enemy Tier 2
- deep side-lane waves
- inhibitor turret

more safely.

However:

one Faelight does NOT cover every flank.

---

# Side-Lane Vision Gaps

Even a powerful side-lane Faelight can leave paths such as:

- approach through the lane itself
- routes behind the split pusher
- alternate jungle corridors
- wall hops
- Teleport wards
- enemy base exits
- paths from the opposite side of jungle

Therefore use:

Faelight
+
minimap information
+
enemy tracking

rather than treating the ward as complete safety.

---

# Split-Push Safety Rule

The more enemies that are missing:

the less aggressively the split pusher should rely on only one Faelight.

Example:

You are pushing enemy Tier 2 Top.

Faelight reveals the nearest jungle path.

Enemy Jungle is visible Bot.
Enemy Support is visible Mid.
Enemy ADC is visible Bot.

Only Top + Mid missing.

This is relatively safe information.

But if:

all 5 enemies are missing

one Faelight is insufficient to justify hitting Tier 2 indefinitely.

---

# Best Control Ward Faelights

The POST-TRANSFORMATION SIDE-LANE FAELIGHTS are often the best Faelight locations for Control Wards when your team owns that side of the map.

Why:

- they are farther from high-traffic central river
- clearing them can require enemies to enter contested side-jungle territory
- they directly protect split pushing
- a long-lived Control Ward repeatedly provides normal ward vision even after the 45-second Faelight bonus ends
- it denies nearby enemy wards while remaining useful for future side-lane pressure

Use a Control Ward here when:

- your split pusher is strong
- your team controls nearby jungle
- the next objective is on the opposite side of the map
- enemies must take risk to walk in and clear it

This can create persistent map pressure.

---

# When NOT to Use a Control Ward on a Side Faelight

Do NOT use one merely because the location is powerful.

A Control Ward is bad if:

- your team has no control of the quadrant
- enemies can walk to it freely
- the lane is pushed toward you
- you are about to abandon that side of the map
- the next major objective requires everyone on the opposite side

In those cases:

use a regular ward

and preserve the Control Ward for objective setup.

---

# Control Ward vs Regular Ward Summary

## Island Brush Faelight

Typical choice:
REGULAR WARD

Control Ward:
only when nearby river is actively controlled.

Reason:
high traffic and relatively easy to clear.

---

## Mid Banana-Brush Faelight

Typical choice:
REGULAR WARD

Control Ward:
situational objective setup.

Reason:
central, commonly swept, often contested.

---

## Base-Gate Faelight

Defending team:
CONTROL WARD can be excellent.

Attacking team:
situational.

Reason:
defenders can protect the location cheaply.

---

## Post-Transformation Side-Lane Faelight

Typical choice when side control exists:
CONTROL WARD can be excellent.

Otherwise:
REGULAR WARD.

Reason:
the enemy may need to enter dangerous side-jungle territory to clear it.

These are generally the highest-value persistent Faelight locations for a split-pushing team.

---

# 45-Second Bonus Vision Window

The special shaped Faelight region lasts:

45 seconds

even if the underlying ward lasts longer.

Therefore the strongest time to make a play is:

immediately after placing the ward.

Example:

ward side Faelight
→ bonus region activates
→ push wave / hit turret during next 45 seconds

Do NOT place the Faelight ward and then spend 30 seconds recalling before trying to use its special vision.

---

# Using Faelights Before Objectives

Faelights can be part of objective setup.

Ideal timing:

approximately 45-60 seconds before the objective.

Example:

Dragon in 50 seconds.

1. Push Mid/Bot.
2. Place relevant river Faelight ward.
3. Use the 45-second bonus vision to identify enemy setup.
4. Clear nearby enemy wards.
5. Move into river.

The bonus vision window closely matches the important pre-objective setup period.

---

# Faelight + Wave Pressure

The strongest use is often:

vision
+
wave pressure

rather than vision alone.

Example:

Side-lane Faelight activated.

Push side wave into Tier 2.

Enemy must now either:

- walk through visible jungle path
- take an alternate route
- give turret damage

The ward creates information that makes the push more reliable.

---

# Faelight + Major Objective Pressure

Example:

Baron spawning soon.

Your team sends Top side-laner Bot.

Place Control Ward on Bot-side post-transformation Faelight.

The split pusher applies pressure.

If enemies rotate Bot:

Faelight may reveal the movement early.

Your team can use that information to:

- start Baron
- take river control
- force a numbers advantage

This is one of the strongest macro applications of Faelight vision.

---

# Playing Against Enemy Faelights

Do NOT always walk directly toward a visible Faelight.

Assume:

the enemy may see farther than the ward's normal radius.

Instead consider:

- alternate paths
- Sweeper
- Scryer's Bloom
- approaching through a vision gap
- waiting until the 45-second bonus region expires
- sending a tankier champion to clear
- clearing only when teammates can cover

---

# Clearing a Control-Warded Faelight

A Control Ward on a strong side-lane Faelight may be intentionally dangerous to clear.

Before walking in:

check:

- enemy split pusher location
- enemy Jungle location
- enemy Mid location
- nearby lane priority
- whether clearing the ward exposes you to collapse

Do NOT trade a champion death for:

30 ward gold.

If the enemy is using the ward to establish pressure:

sometimes it is better to surrender the area temporarily and clear it after pushing the lane or gaining numbers.

---

# Stealth Ward Trinket

Patch 26.1 initially reduced Stealth Ward cooldown from:

210-120 seconds

to:

170-90 seconds

by level.

Patch 26.3 partially reverted the early-game portion because early Faelight vision was suppressing junglers too heavily.

Current Stealth Ward cooldown:

210-90 seconds

by level.

Therefore:

early laners have less Faelight uptime than they did at Season 16 launch.

Late-game vision access remains improved.

---

# Oracle Lens

Oracle Lens duration:

8 seconds

Season 16 increased this from:

6 seconds.

This helps players clear:

- normal wards
- Control Wards
- detected Faelight Superwards

Because Faelight wards can cover unusually large areas, Sweeper is especially important for:

- ganks
- side-lane collapses
- objective setup

---

# Scryer's Bloom

Normal Scryer's Bloom respawn:

200-260 seconds

randomized.

Season 16 reduced this from:

260-350 seconds.

This gives teams more frequent access to long-range vision clearing.

---

# Additional Scryer's Bloom Locations

At:

15:00
or
Elemental Rift transformation

additional Scryer's Bloom locations become available.

These include:

- one near each side-lane river coast
- locations behind side-lane inner turrets
- locations near base rocks

The new locations have much shorter respawn timers:

90-120 seconds

These are particularly useful for:

- safely entering side jungle
- clearing Faelight wards
- protecting split pushes
- recovering vision when behind

---

# Faelight + Scryer's Bloom Interaction

A useful anti-Faelight sequence:

Scryer's Bloom
→ reveal ward
→ Sweeper / approach
→ clear Faelight ward

This allows a team to dismantle side-lane vision without blindly face-checking.

---

# Vision Before Side-Laning

Before pushing past river:

try to know at least:

- enemy Jungle position
- enemy Mid position
- nearest likely collapse route

Faelight vision can provide one part of this information.

It should not be the only information source.

---

# Vision Before Dragon

Useful areas:

- river entrances
- Bot-side jungle approaches
- enemy Jungle entry paths
- river Faelights
- nearby brush
- Dragon pit

One Faelight alone does NOT control Dragon.

Patch 26.3 specifically reduced Mid Faelight coverage near the objective pit.

---

# Vision Before Baron

Useful areas:

- river
- enemy Top jungle entrances
- blast-cone access
- pit
- approaches from Mid
- side-lane jungle paths

If your team controls Baron:

a deep side-lane Control Ward may be useful because it can identify enemies responding to split pressure instead of approaching Baron.

---

# Vision and Gank Safety

Never say:

"This Faelight means you cannot be ganked."

Instead classify risk.

## LOW RISK

- relevant Faelight active
- Jungle visible elsewhere
- nearby laners visible
- wave positioned safely

## MODERATE RISK

- Faelight active
- Jungle missing
- one alternate route unwarded

## HIGH RISK

- multiple enemies missing
- wave deeply pushed
- Faelight's 45-second region expired
- alternate jungle/river routes unwarded

Faelights reduce uncertainty.

They do not remove it.

---

# Important Patch 26.3 Change

At Season 16 launch:

Mid-river Faelights provided too much early vision.

They could see:

past Scuttle
toward epic-pit entrances.

Patch 26.3 changed the region so it now ends:

before Scuttle pad
and
before objective-pit entrance.

This was specifically done because the original Faelights made early Jungle approaches too easy to detect.

Therefore any coaching based on launch-day Mid Faelight coverage is outdated.

---

# Faelight Macro Decision Framework

Before placing a Faelight ward, ask:

1. What play am I trying to make?
2. Which enemy path matters most?
3. Is the 45-second bonus vision useful immediately?
4. Can the enemy clear the ward safely?
5. Should this be a Stealth Ward or Control Ward?
6. What routes remain unseen?
7. What objective or turret pressure does this vision enable?

Use the ward to enable a specific action.

Do not ward simply because a Faelight exists.

---

# Final Faelight Rule

Faelights are strongest when they convert vision into:

- a safer split push
- a turret
- an objective setup
- a pick
- a jungle invade
- a protected retreat

The best Faelight is not necessarily the one that reveals the largest area.

The best Faelight is the one that reveals the path the enemy must use to stop your current play.
