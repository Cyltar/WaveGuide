# Season 16 Macro Decision-Making

Last updated: September 2026
Applies to: Standard Summoner's Rift

# Quick Reference

- Macro decisions should compare the total expected value of available plays rather than following fixed rules such as "always group for Dragon."
- Evaluate: gold gained, experience gained, waves lost/denied, structures, objective value, quest progress, vision, map access, tempo, death risk, and what the enemy gains simultaneously.
- Before rotating, ask what you are giving up by leaving your current position and what your arrival actually changes.
- Before staying in a side lane, ask what the enemy can obtain while you remain there and whether you can convert your pressure quickly enough.
- Turret pressure should include obtainable plate gold, turret gold, denied waves, and permanent map access.
- Role Quest progress can change the value of early roaming, lane swaps, and abandoning lane; completing a quest may be worth prioritizing before a marginal play elsewhere.
- Faelight and ward coverage determine how far a player can safely extend; identify uncovered enemy paths rather than treating vision as binary.
- Objective decisions depend on lane priority, vision, current strength, composition, enemy position, Smite, objective rewards, Soul state, and available cross-map trades.
- Herald is especially valuable when it converts pressure into structures or forces an enemy response before another major objective.
- Baron can be superior to Dragon when your team has a safe Baron window and the immediate Dragon/Soul value does not outweigh Baron pressure.
- Objective bounties increase the value of otherwise ordinary cross-map resources for the losing team and can make nominally equal trades unequal.
- A losing team should maximize bounty windows without sacrificing structures whose loss would allow the enemy to immediately end the game.
- Tempo matters: a play that earns slightly less raw gold can be superior if it creates an earlier recall, first move, vision setup, objective position, or numbers advantage.
- Do not evaluate a play only by what your team gains; subtract what the enemy gains during the same time window.
- When several choices are close, prefer the play with reliable value and a safe exit over a theoretically higher-value play requiring the enemy not to respond.
- Use the specialized knowledge files for exact Season 16 mechanics and numbers; use this file primarily to combine those mechanics into macro decisions.

# Purpose

This file is the decision-making framework for Season 16 macro.

Use the other Season 16 knowledge files as factual sources for:

- objective timers and rewards

# Core Macro Principle

Every action has an opportunity cost.

If a player:

- roams
- recalls
- groups
- starts an objective
- invades
- pushes a turret
- catches a wave
- sets vision

they are giving up some other possible action.

Therefore macro advice should answer:

"What do I gain?"

"What do I give up?"

"What is the probability the play succeeds?"

"What does this create 30-90 seconds later?"

Examples:

- Dragon stacks

## Temporary Power

Examples:

- temporary vision control

## Map Control

Examples:

Examples:

# Expected Value Framework

When useful, reason approximately as:

Expected Play Value =
Probability of Success
×
Reward if Successful
-
Cost if Failed
-
Guaranteed Resources Given Up

This does NOT require exact mathematical calculation in every answer.

Use it as a reasoning framework.

# Example

Option A:

Rotate to a 50/50 Dragon fight.

Potential reward:
- Dragon
- kills

Possible cost:
- death
- lost Top wave
- lost Tier 2 plates
- delayed Top quest

Option B:

Stay Top.

Guaranteed:
- 2 Tier 2 plates = 240 local gold
- wave gold
- wave XP
- Role Quest progress

If Dragon is only an early individual Dragon and the rotation is low probability:

Option B may be better.

If Dragon gives enemy Soul:

# WAVES ARE THE FOUNDATION OF MACRO

Most good macro begins with wave management.

Before:

- roaming
- recalling
- warding
- invading
- grouping
- starting objectives

ask:

"What happens to my wave?"

---

# Push Before Moving

Default pattern:

push
→
move

Benefits:

- enemy must collect the wave
- you lose fewer minions
- enemy turret takes pressure
- you create priority
- enemy movement becomes more costly

This applies especially before:

- Dragon
- Grubs
- Herald
- Baron
- river fights

---

# When NOT to Push First

Immediate decisive events can override wave optimization.

Examples:

- nearby teammate being collapsed on
- enemy jungler caught
- Baron already at low HP
- Elder fight beginning
- game-ending fight

Do not finish a wave while your team loses the game beside you.

---

# Lane Priority

Priority means you can move before the opponent without suffering an unacceptable loss.

Priority is strongest when:

- enemy wave is under turret
- enemy must last-hit
- enemy recently recalled
- enemy is low HP or mana
- enemy cannot safely contest your push

Priority is NOT simply:

"I am standing farther forward."

---

# Priority and Objectives

Before an objective:

Mid priority often matters most because Mid has access to both sides of the river.

For Dragon:

Mid + Bot priority are especially valuable.

For Grubs / Herald / Baron:

Mid + Top priority are especially valuable.

If both adjacent lanes are pushed into your team:

starting the objective is significantly riskier.

---

# WAVE DENIAL

A play becomes more valuable if it also causes the enemy to lose a wave.

Example:

Push Mid into enemy turret.

Then move Dragon.

Enemy Mid must choose:

catch wave
or
move immediately.

If they move:

they lose gold and XP.

If they stay:

your team gains numbers / setup advantage.

This is one of the strongest forms of tempo.

---

# SIDE WAVES BEFORE OBJECTIVES

Approximately 45-90 seconds before a major objective:

prepare side waves.

Ideal:

push side waves outward
→
recall
→
move objective

This forces the enemy to respond while your team sets up.

---

# RECALL TIMING

A good recall creates a spending and movement advantage.

Recall when:

- wave is crashed
- objective is spawning soon
- meaningful gold can be spent
- health / mana are low
- staying creates unnecessary risk

---

# Bad Recall Timing

Avoid recalling:

- exactly when Dragon spawns
- while a large wave crashes into your turret
- while enemy is taking a free structure
- after your team already entered river without you

---

# Pre-Objective Recall

Ideal sequence:

Objective in ~60-90 seconds

1. Push wave.
2. Recall.
3. Spend gold.
4. Refill HP / mana.
5. Move early.
6. Establish vision.

This is generally better than:

stay for one extra wave
→
recall late
→
arrive after enemy controls river

# SEASON 16 HOMEGUARD

Season 16 significantly improved Homeguard.

Homeguard no longer has a fixed duration.

It lasts until:

- reaching its endpoint
- entering combat
- entering the jungle

Before 14:00:

80% bonus movement speed
decaying to
40%
over 4 seconds

After 14:00:

150%
decaying to
65%
over 4 seconds

After 14:00, or after a turret has been destroyed in that lane:

Homeguard tracks the furthest allied minion wave

and ends approximately:

2000 units before the wave.

This makes later recalls less punishing than in older seasons and allows faster re-entry into side lanes. Riot explicitly changed Homeguard to reduce downtime and enable more proactive plays. citeturn805880search0

# Homeguard Macro Implication

A player can sometimes:

push
→
recall
→
buy
→
Homeguard back toward the wave

without losing as much pressure as older League knowledge might suggest.

This particularly matters:

- after 14:00
- in long side lanes
- when preparing Baron / Dragon
- when defending split pressure

Do NOT automatically say:

"If you recall now, you will lose all side pressure."

Season 16 Homeguard can substantially reduce that cost.

# Quest-Lane Passive Progress

For Top / Mid / Bot:

In assigned lane:

1.5 quest points / second
≈ 90 per minute

Outside assigned lane without stored roaming time:

0.333 / second
≈ 20 per minute

Difference:

≈ 70 points per minute

Therefore leaving lane has a measurable quest cost.

---

# Stored Roaming Time

After level 3:

2 seconds in lane
≈
1 second banked roaming

Maximum:

60 seconds

Therefore:

120 seconds in lane
can bank
~60 seconds of full-rate roaming.

This means the correct advice is NOT:

"Never roam before quest."

Instead:

push
→
bank roam time
→
use high-value roam
→
return

1200 points

Important Top quest values:

- 2 per minion
- 15 champion takedown

Passive lane rate:

# Top Quest Tradeoff Example

Top can either:

A:
roam for a speculative kill

or

B:
stay one minute and take one plate

B gives roughly:

90 passive points
+
40 plate points
+
minion points

potentially:

130+ quest points

before wave CS.

One champion takedown:

15 points.

Therefore roaming purely for quest progress is usually inefficient.

The roam must be justified by:

- actual kill gold
- objective value
- map pressure

not just quest points.

# MID QUEST MACRO

Mid completion:

1350

Mid gains:

- 2/minion
- 25/takedown
- 30 epic
- 40 plate
- 50 turret
- champion-damage quest progress

Mid is more naturally rewarded for fighting and roaming.

Therefore successful Mid roams can be much more quest-efficient than Top roams.

Still:

push first whenever practical.

# ADC QUEST MACRO

Bot completion:

1350

Bot gains:

3 quest points per minion.

One normal 6-minion wave:

18 quest points

One champion takedown:

15

Therefore a full wave provides more raw quest points from CS alone than one champion takedown.

This is before:

- wave gold
- XP
- passive quest generation

So ADC should not abandon multiple waves for speculative early fights.

# SUPPORT MACRO

Patch 26.16 heavily penalized extremely early roaming.

Outside Bot until level 5:

-33% minion gold
-33% minion XP

Champion/turret-damage support quest stacking was also reduced / equalized with minion execution.

Therefore:

Level 2-4 Support roaming should have a clear purpose.

Good:

- immediate jungle fight
- strong roam timer
- objective contest
- enemy overextension

Bad:

- permanently abandoning ADC
- wandering Mid with no setup
- functioning as a second jungler by default

# Mid Tier 1 Map Value

Mid Tier 1 is often strategically more important than another lane's Tier 1 because destroying it opens:

- both river entrances
- deeper jungle access
- easier rotations
- easier Dragon setup
- easier Baron setup

# INHIBITOR VALUE

Direct inhibitor gold is small.

Strategic value is large.

Destroying inhibitor creates:

super minions

This can create:

- Baron setup

- 5v4 map situations

# When NOT to Take Inhibitor

Sometimes taking inhibitor too early can provide enemies with safer access to super-minion farm near their base.

If:

- no major objective is coming
- your team cannot exploit pressure
- inhibitor will respawn before relevant objective

consider whether delaying the inhibitor has strategic merit.

Do NOT make this an absolute rule.

The value depends on:

- Baron timing
- Dragon timing
- map control
- ability to siege

# SPLIT PUSHING

# 4-1 Pressure

Four players pressure one area.

One strong side-laner pressures another.

Goal:

force enemy to choose.

If 2 enemies answer split:

main group has 4v3.

If 1 answers:

splitter may continue if winning matchup.

---

# 1-3-1

Two side lanes pressured.

Three players control mid.

Higher coordination required.

Strong when:

- side-laners have mobility / Teleport
- team controls vision
- enemy waveclear cannot answer all lanes

---

# Split Push Before Baron

Example:

Baron alive.

Split pusher Bot.

Baron becomes a strong option.

splitter takes turret.

# OBJECTIVE SETUP

Start setup:

45-90 seconds before important objective.

Sequence:

1. Push nearby lanes.
2. Recall.
3. Spend gold.
4. Move first.
5. Establish Faelight / ward vision.
6. Clear enemy vision.
7. Control entrances.
8. Start only if favorable.

# Objective Setup Failure

Bad sequence:

objective spawns
→
recall
→
walk through dark river
→

This is often a tempo loss before the fight even begins.

# VISION CONTROL

Vision is most valuable when tied to an action.

Before aggressive play, identify:

1. enemy jungler

4. Teleport users

- push waves

- protect shutdowns

- random fights
- unnecessary dives

Season 16 makes turret pressure especially valuable.

After winning fight:

do not automatically recall.

- plate
- Tier 2

# PLAYING BEHIND

- safe waves
